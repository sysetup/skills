# Literature and theory

Goal of the review is a **gap argument**, not a book report. This file covers literature as a step in every study, and literature review as the study itself (estado del arte, scoping, systematic).

## Topic statement

Write one working title and one topic sentence that names population, phenomenon or variables, and setting. Revise after the first search pass.

## Search protocol (desk research)

Adapted from PRISMA 2020 (Page et al., *BMJ* 2021; doi:10.1136/bmj.n71). This is a four-phase **audit trail** for agent desk research, not a claim of a full systematic review unless the user asked for one.

Write 5–12 search strings (synonyms, antonyms, field terms). Record tool or database, date of search, and any date or language limits. Prefer peer-reviewed, government, and primary data. Tag blogs, vendor pages, and undated posts as weak.

Stop when new sources repeat the same claims (saturation of the map), not when a page quota is hit. Saturation does not skip the count table.

### Four-phase flow (record n at each box)

**Identification**
- Records identified from search tools or databases (n=), by string.
- Records identified from other sources (citation chasing, user files, registers) (n=).
- Records removed before screening: duplicates and out-of-scope by automated rule (n=).

**Screening**
- Records screened at title/abstract or equivalent (n=).
- Records excluded at this stage (n=), with aggregated reasons (wrong population, not empirical, off-topic).

**Eligibility**
- Reports sought for retrieval (n=).
- Reports not retrieved (n=) — paywall, dead link, unverified bibliographic entry. Do not cite what was not retrieved.
- Reports assessed in full (n=).
- Reports excluded (n=), each with a specific reason (wrong design, no usable data, duplicate publication of the same study).

**Inclusion**
- Studies / sources included in the review (n=).
- If several reports belong to one study, count studies and reports separately.

Copy this count table into the execution log. A claimed systematic review also needs explicit eligibility criteria, who screened, and disagreement handling. An ordinary gap-oriented review still ships the four counts.

### Source log (each included record)

Source, year, design, sample or corpus, main claim, limitation.

## How the literature is used (Creswell)

- **Quantitative / mixed (quant-heavy)** — literature frames the problem, justifies variables, and leads to questions or hypotheses. Place the bulk of the review up front.
- **Qualitative** — literature orients the problem but stays light enough that participant meanings can still lead. Theory may appear late.
- **All approaches** — use literature to show deficiencies (topics omitted, methods weak, populations missing, voices unheard).
- **DSRM** — literature motivates the problem and shows that existing artifacts are missing or insufficient. It is not a substitute for demonstration and evaluation (`references/dsrm.md`).
- **Literature-review study** — literature *is* the data. Do not relabel it mixed methods. Findings report what prior work claims and where it is deficient; they are not this project's experimental, survey, or phenomenological result (`references/inference-license.md`).

## Literature review as the study (estado del arte)

Use when the user asks for estado del arte, systematic review, scoping review, or "lit review for a thesis on X", and no new human or field data will be collected.

- Approach = structured inquiry. Do not force experimental or phenomenological labels onto a review.
- Worldview = usually postpositivist (transparent protocol, replicable counts) or pragmatic (policy/thesis synthesis). Constructivist only if the product is an interpretive qualitative metasynthesis and you say so.
- Design name = narrative review, scoping review, or systematic review. Systematic only if eligibility criteria, dual-screening logic, and PRISMA counts are actually run. Default thesis "estado del arte" = gap-oriented narrative or scoping review with PRISMA-adapted counts — not a fake Cochrane review.
- Purpose script: `The purpose of this {narrative / scoping / systematic} review is to {map / synthesize / appraise} {body of evidence} on {phenomenon or variables} in {population / setting} in order to {name the gap this thesis or study will fill}.`
- Review questions (write 1–3). PICO when the literature is intervention-effectiveness; SPIDER when it is qualitative meaning; a concept–population–context question for scoping.
- Methods: search strings, sources, years, languages, inclusion/exclusion, quality appraisal rule (design fit + risk of bias in plain language; name a tool if used, e.g. a checklist — do not fake scores).
- Analysis: cluster the included studies by design, population, construct, and finding. Argue 3–6 deficiencies. End with the implication for the next empirical study (purpose + candidate design), not with "more research is needed."
- Validation: source-quality ranking, unrecovered-source log, contrary-string search, algorithmic-instrument threats.
- Artifacts: count table, source log, cluster map, deficiency list, consistency matrix with the review questions as the RQ column, timeline of the search.

## Deficiency-in-the-evidence move

After summarizing clusters, write 3–6 concrete deficiencies. Each deficiency must point to a need this study can address. Ban vague "more research is needed."

## Theory placement

- **Quantitative** — independent, dependent, mediating, moderating variables. State the theory, the propositions, and where it sits (introduction vs dedicated section). Hypotheses must be traceable to the theory or to a well-specified empirical model.
- **Qualitative** — theory as lens, as end-product (grounded theory), or as sensitizing concepts. Name the choice. Do not force a grand theory onto a phenomenological study.
- **Mixed** — social-science theory, transformative framework, or a combination. State which strand the theory governs and how integration uses it.

## Definition of terms

Define only technical or contested terms as they will be used in this study. Operationalize quantitative variables. Keep qualitative definitions provisional when the field will refine them.

## Agent outputs for this step

- Working title
- Clustered literature map (not an annotated dump)
- PRISMA-adapted count table (four phases)
- Deficiency list
- Theory statement and variable or concept map
- Revised problem paragraph (hook + evidence of the problem + deficiencies + significance)
- For a review study: review questions, appraisal rule, and implication for the next empirical design
